# ICP

## Engagement signals
What makes a contractor a great fit. 3-7 bullets.
- Hiring field techs or dispatchers - growing capacity that needs filling
- No online booking; the site is a phone number and a contact form at best
- Booking widget or website visibly years out of date
- Review count climbing (demand is there) but response time is slow
- Owner-operated and past the one-person stage, so an office process can stick

## Anti-signals
What makes you walk away. 3-5 bullets.
- National franchise with corporate-run marketing and a locked tech stack
- One-person shop with no capacity to take more work
- Commercial-contract-only, no consumer/residential booking flow
- Wants guaranteed lead volume before any pilot
- No website and no intent to build any online presence

## Disqualifying conditions
Hard exclusions. The deal does not happen if any of these are true.
- Unlicensed operator (not on the provincial trade-licensing registry)
- No service capacity to add jobs (fully booked, not hiring)
- No budget authority in the room

## Buying committee
Who actually signs off + who blocks.
- Decision maker: Owner / Operator
- Champion: Office Manager who lives in the scheduling pain
- Blocker: an incumbent marketing agency already on retainer
